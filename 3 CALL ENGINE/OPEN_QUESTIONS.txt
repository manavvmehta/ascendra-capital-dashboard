================================================================================
OPEN QUESTIONS — THE EQUITY CALL ENGINE
================================================================================

Every one of these is a choice someone had to make, made without evidence.
They are in the live code now, shaping real calls.

Change ONE thing at a time and report each separately, or there is no way to
tell which change did what.


Q1.  THE SCORE DOES NOT WORK.  <- start here
     A confluence score decides what fires (>= 6.5) and what is
     silently filed as "watching". Across 479 resolved calls its rank
     correlation with R is near zero.
     It is also confounded: SQUEEZE only ever publishes at one score, and the
     highest scores are almost all BREAKOUT-20D, so a high-vs-low comparison
     compares setups rather than conviction.
     ASK: rebuild it, re-weight it per setup, or abandon it for a simple
     per-setup filter? If the score has no signal, what should gate instead?

Q2.  THE SHORTLIST IS UNVALIDATED.
     Only the top 25 names by a cheap snapshot formula ever get
     15-minute candles. Everything else is invisible, however good its setup.
     The formula: relative volume x2, plus range/ATR, plus |%change| x0.3,
     plus how close price sits to the day's extreme.
     ASK: what is that costing? Is relative volume the right leading term?
     Should the count be larger?

Q3.  PULLBACK IS THE WEAKEST SETUP AND HAS THE SOFTEST GATE.
     +0.068R over 125 trades, confidence interval crossing zero. It is also
     the only setup where participation merely scores instead of blocking.
     ASK: should it gate at 1.1x like the
     others? Or is the pullback simply not an edge?

Q4.  THE SWING STOP.
     BREAKOUT-20D uses 2x daily ATR, tightened to just under the broken level.
     It has the widest outcome spread of any setup (sd 1.62 against ~1.0 for
     the intraday ones) and only a 41% win rate.
     ASK: is 2x ATR right for a swing breakout, or should the stop sit under
     the level only? What does that do to the spread?

Q5.  NO MARKET CONTEXT ANYWHERE.
     Every call is judged in isolation. A long fires the same way whether the
     index is up 1% or down 2%.
     ASK: should longs be suppressed in a falling market — and can that be
     shown, or is it curve-fitting? The `mkt_nifty_pts` and `mkt_flow` columns
     record the market state at signal time, so it is testable.

Q6.  THE TIME EXIT.
     Intraday calls are closed at 15:10 regardless. Chosen to stay clear of
     the closing auction.
     ASK: is that leaving money on the table or saving it? TIME_EXIT is
     the second most common outcome in the data, so it matters.

Q7.  ORB HAS ALMOST NO SAMPLE.
     Five trades. Its window (09:45-11:30) and its opening-range width
     constraint (0.2 to 0.9 of daily ATR) are both narrow.
     ASK: are those constraints right, or are they starving a good setup?

Q8.  THE PARTICIPATION THRESHOLDS.
     1.1x / 1.3x / 1.5x / 2.5x / 1.2x, per setup, set by judgement.
     The 2.5x on BREAKOUT-20D has one piece of evidence behind it: over three
     weeks, breakouts under 2.5x went 0 wins / 7 stops. The rest have none.
     ASK: are they in the right places?


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO WORK WITH calls.csv
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  624 rows, 51 columns, 21 Jul - 1 Sep 2026. 479 have a final R.

  status      T2_HIT, T1_TRAIL_OUT, SL_HIT, TIME_EXIT are resolved.
              EXPIRED means the entry was never reached — not a loss, a
              non-event. VOID_* means tracking failed; exclude those.
  r           the outcome in R. -1.0 is a full stop.
  score       what fired it. >= 6.5 published.
  comp_*      the score broken into its parts. Only on schema_version 2.
  instr_*     measurements at signal time — participation, ATR, ADX, RSI,
              distance to VWAP and EMA20. Only on schema_version 2.

  RISK IS entry MINUS risk0. Do NOT use the `sl` column for that: the engine
  moves the stop to breakeven after T1, so on any row that reached T1 the
  stored stop is not the stop the trade was taken with. This has already
  produced one wrong analysis.

  REFUSE TO CONCLUDE ON n < 30. A documented finding here ("the 8.0 bucket
  underperforms 7.0") was repeated for weeks and dissolved on a proper sample:
  -0.138R with a 95% interval of [-0.568, +0.281].

  analysis_score_check.py is the script that found that. Read it before
  writing your own — it already handles the bucketing, the confidence
  intervals and the per-setup confound.
