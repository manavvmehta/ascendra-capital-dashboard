PART 1  ·  WHAT THIS IS AND WHY IT EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A live scanner runs daily charts for the inverted head-and-shoulders pattern and paper-trades
every one it finds with Rs2,00,000 of virtual money.

Nothing is executed. It is a measurement instrument. The purpose is to find out
whether the pattern works, on his rules, before real money is involved.

THE WHOLE THING WAS BUILT FROM ONE CHART.
    STLTECH (Sterlite Technologies), NSE, daily. Pattern formed June-August
    2026, broke out late August.

        Left shoulder low     553.25   (11 June 2026)
        Head low              492.25   (14 July 2026)
        Right shoulder low    601.00   (21 August 2026)
        Neckline              683.50   (horizontal)
        Breakout close        695.85   (27 August 2026)
        Breakout volume       1.09x the 20-day average

    Every threshold in the detector was reverse-engineered so that this one
    chart passes. That is a sample size of one. It is the main reason this
    handoff exists.

THE ORIGINAL INSTRUCTION, VERBATIM
    "there is one pattern called inverted head and shoulder, spot that pattern
    on any chart on daily time frame and the final candle should break the
    resistance level with good volume... there are two targets in this pattern.
    first target is the length of the shoulder and the second target is the
    head. it is not necessary that the length of the shoulders should be same,
    minor difference in the length of the shoulders is totally fine."

A LATER CORRECTION, VERBATIM (01/09/2026)
    "both the ends of the head should be almost equal or 2-5% price difference
    is fine, the chart that you picked had a lot of price difference."

    This produced NECK_TOL = 0.05 in the code below.

A DELIBERATE REMOVAL, VERBATIM
    "lets skip that volume indicater for this tab"

    So volume is measured and reported but gates nothing. Note the tension:
    the original description asked for "good volume", and the reference
    chart broke out on only 1.09x. Question Q10 in PART 5 is about this.

PART 2  ·  THE DETECTOR, VERBATIM AND RUNNABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the exact source running in production. It is fully standalone - it
imports only `datetime` from the standard library. Save it as inv_hs.py and it
will run against any list of daily OHLC bars.

INPUT FORMAT
    A list of dicts, oldest first:
        {"date": "2026-08-27", "open": 690.0, "high": 700.0,
          "low": 685.0, "close": 695.85, "volume": 2028680}

USAGE
    import inv_hs as H
    pattern = H.find(bars, "STLTECH")         # None if no shape
    ok, why = H.qualifies(pattern)            # is it a FRESH breakout?

`find` returns the best-scoring pattern in the window, or None. `qualifies`
tells you whether it has broken out recently enough to act on.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
inv_hs.py — inverted head-and-shoulders detection on daily candles.

DETECTION ONLY. It finds the shape and reports it. No stop, no target, no
position, no book. Each one is analysed by hand (owner's instruction, 31/08).

THE SHAPE (his description, STLTECH example)
    three swing lows — LOW, LOWER, LOW — with the middle one (the HEAD) the
    deepest, and the two outer ones (the SHOULDERS) roughly level. The two
    rallies between them peak at similar highs; the line across those peaks is
    the NECKLINE. The pattern completes when a candle CLOSES above the neckline
    on strong volume.

    "it is not necessary that the length of the shoulder should be same, minor
     difference in the length of the shoulders is totally fine"

    So symmetry is checked loosely — see SHOULDER_TOL.

TARGETS (reported as information, not traded)
    T1  neckline + (neckline - shoulder low)
    T2  neckline + (neckline - head low)
    Which shoulder feeds T1 is ambiguous when they differ, so BOTH are shown
    rather than one being silently chosen.

WHY THE PIVOT RULE IS THE WHOLE GAME
    Every parameter below is a way to find a pattern that is not there. PIVOT_K
    especially: too small and every wiggle is a shoulder, too large and nothing
    is ever found. It is set once, here, and applied to every stock — not tuned
    per name, which is how a detector becomes a curve-fitter.
"""
from datetime import date

# ── the only knobs, all in one place ───────────────────────────────────────
PIVOT_K = 5          # a swing low is the lowest close/low within +/- K bars
LOOKBACK = 200       # daily bars examined
MIN_HEAD_EDGE = 0.03  # the head must be >=3% below the shallower shoulder
SHOULDER_TOL = 0.60  # shoulders' depths may differ by up to 60% of the deeper
NECK_TOL = 0.05      # the two peaks either side of the head — the ENDS of the
                     # head — may differ by at most 5%. His words, 01/09:
                     # "almost equal or 2-5% price difference is fine".
                     # This is a hard structural condition, not a preference:
                     # without it the neckline can be drawn far above one side
                     # of the pattern and the shape is not an inverted H&S.
MIN_DEPTH_PCT = 0.04  # head must sit >=4% below the neckline — no flat noise
MAX_BARS = 160       # the whole pattern must fit in this many bars
MIN_BARS = 20        # ...and take at least this many
# NO VOLUME GATE (31/08). It was 1.5x the 20-day average, and it would have
# hidden the reference example: STLTECH broke out on 2,028,680 against a
# 1,861,817 average — 1.09x — and ran +6.3% in two sessions anyway.
#
# The multiple is still measured and printed on every row. It is just not
# allowed to decide what he sees, because he is judging these by hand and a
# threshold I invented should not be filtering his candidates.
FRESH_BARS = 3       # the breakout close must be within this many bars of now


def pivots(bars, k=PIVOT_K):
    """(lows, highs) as lists of (index, price).

    A pivot low is strictly the lowest LOW in the window either side. Ties are
    rejected on purpose: a flat base produces dozens of equal lows and every
    one of them would become a candidate shoulder.
    """
    lo, hi = [], []
    for i in range(k, len(bars) - k):
        w = bars[i - k:i + k + 1]
        if bars[i]["low"] < min(b["low"] for b in w if b is not bars[i]):
            lo.append((i, bars[i]["low"]))
        if bars[i]["high"] > max(b["high"] for b in w if b is not bars[i]):
            hi.append((i, bars[i]["high"]))
    return lo, hi


def find(bars, symbol=""):
    """Return the best inverted H&S in `bars`, or None.

    `bars` is oldest-first, each {date, open, high, low, close, volume}.
    """
    if len(bars) < MIN_BARS + 2 * PIVOT_K:
        return None
    lows, highs = pivots(bars)
    if len(lows) < 3 or len(highs) < 2:
        return None

    best = None
    for a in range(len(lows) - 2):
        for b in range(a + 1, len(lows) - 1):
            for c in range(b + 1, len(lows)):
                iL, pL = lows[a]          # left shoulder
                iH, pH = lows[b]          # head
                iR, pR = lows[c]          # right shoulder
                span = iR - iL
                if not (MIN_BARS <= span <= MAX_BARS):
                    continue
                # The head must be the deepest, and clearly so. ONE check, not
                # two: `pH >= pL or pH >= pR` was here as well and is entirely
                # subsumed — if pH >= min(pL,pR) then pH > min(pL,pR)*(1-edge)
                # follows automatically. It was dead code, and no mutation of it
                # could ever be caught, which is how it was found.
                if pH > min(pL, pR) * (1 - MIN_HEAD_EDGE):
                    continue
                # the two peaks BETWEEN the lows form the neckline
                h1 = [p for (i, p) in highs if iL < i < iH]
                h2 = [p for (i, p) in highs if iH < i < iR]
                if not h1 or not h2:
                    continue
                # Horizontal neckline at the HIGHER peak. That is how it was drawn
                # it on STLTECH, and it is the conservative choice: a close above
                # the higher peak is unambiguous, a sloping line is not.
                # THE TWO ENDS OF THE HEAD MUST BE LEVEL.
                #
                # Owner, 01/09: "both the ends of the head should be almost
                # equal or 2-5% price difference is fine, the chart that you
                # picked had a lot of price difference."
                #
                # Until now the neckline was simply the HIGHER of the two
                # peaks, with nothing requiring them to be near each other. A
                # left peak at 650 against a right peak at 500 passed happily,
                # and the neckline was drawn 30% above one side of the head.
                # That is not the pattern — on his STLTECH reference the two
                # peaks sit almost exactly level and the neckline is flat.
                p1, p2 = max(h1), max(h2)
                if p1 <= 0 or p2 <= 0:
                    continue
                neck_gap = abs(p1 - p2) / max(p1, p2)
                if neck_gap > NECK_TOL:
                    continue
                neck = max(p1, p2)
                if neck <= 0:
                    continue
                # depth below the neckline
                dL, dR, dH = neck - pL, neck - pR, neck - pH
                if dH / neck < MIN_DEPTH_PCT:
                    continue
                if dL <= 0 or dR <= 0:
                    continue
                # shoulders roughly level — loosely, as he asked
                if abs(dL - dR) / max(dL, dR) > SHOULDER_TOL:
                    continue
                # score: prefer deep, symmetric, recent
                sym = 1 - abs(dL - dR) / max(dL, dR)
                score = (dH / neck) * 100 * sym + iR * 0.01
                if best is None or score > best["score"]:
                    best = {"score": score, "symbol": symbol,
                            "i_left": iL, "i_head": iH, "i_right": iR,
                            "left": pL, "head": pH, "right": pR, "neck": neck,
                            "d_left": dL, "d_right": dR, "d_head": dH,
                            # kept so the flatness of the neckline is visible
                            # rather than merely enforced
                            "neck_left": round(p1, 2), "neck_right": round(p2, 2),
                            "neck_gap_pct": round(neck_gap * 100, 2),
                            "sym_pct": round(sym * 100, 1),
                            "bars": span}
    if best is None:
        return None

    # ── has it broken out? ──
    tail = bars[best["i_right"]:]
    neck = best["neck"]
    brk = None
    for j, bar in enumerate(tail):
        if bar["close"] > neck:
            idx = best["i_right"] + j
            v20 = [x["volume"] for x in bars[max(0, idx - 20):idx] if x.get("volume")]
            avg = (sum(v20) / len(v20)) if v20 else 0
            brk = {"i": idx, "date": str(bar["date"])[:10], "close": bar["close"],
                   "volume": bar.get("volume") or 0, "vol_avg20": round(avg),
                   "vol_x": round((bar.get("volume") or 0) / avg, 2) if avg else None,
                   "bars_ago": len(bars) - 1 - idx}
            break

    best.update({
        "date_left": str(bars[best["i_left"]]["date"])[:10],
        "date_head": str(bars[best["i_head"]]["date"])[:10],
        "date_right": str(bars[best["i_right"]]["date"])[:10],
        "last_close": bars[-1]["close"],
        "breakout": brk,
        # reported, never traded — and BOTH shoulders, because which one feeds
        # T1 is genuinely ambiguous when they differ
        "t1_right": round(neck + best["d_right"], 2),
        "t1_left": round(neck + best["d_left"], 2),
        "t2_head": round(neck + best["d_head"], 2),
    })
    return best


def qualifies(p, fresh=FRESH_BARS):
    """A fresh close above the neckline. That is the whole test.

    Volume is measured and reported but does NOT gate — see the note above.
    """
    b = p.get("breakout")
    if not b:
        return False, "no close above the neckline yet"
    if b["bars_ago"] > fresh:
        return False, f"broke out {b['bars_ago']} bars ago — not fresh"
    v = f" on {b['vol_x']}x volume" if b.get("vol_x") else ""
    return True, f"closed above {p['neck']:.2f}{v}"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3  ·  THE PAPER-TRADE RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The detector finds shapes. These rules turn a shape into a trade. They live in
a separate module which is NOT included here because it carries application
plumbing, but the logic is complete below - reimplement it exactly.

LEVELS  (computed from the pattern alone, independent of the entry price)

    neck   = the pattern's neckline
    stop   = the RIGHT shoulder low
    T1     = neck + (neck - right shoulder low)
    T2     = neck + (neck - head low)

    Also recorded but NOT traded:
    t1_left_alt = neck + (neck - left shoulder low)

    On STLTECH:  stop 601.00 · T1 766.00 · T2 874.75 · (left alt T1 813.75)

ENTRY
    The NEXT day's OPEN after the breakout close.
    NOT the breakout close itself - that price was not available to act on,
    because the signal only exists once the daily candle has closed.

    If that open is at or below the stop, the trade is SKIPPED entirely. The
    pattern was invalidated overnight and there is no position to take.

SIZE
    floor(Rs2,00,000 / entry price) whole shares.

MANAGEMENT, one daily bar at a time:

    1. If low <= stop:
           if half was already booked at T1:
               exit the remainder at the stop (which is now the entry price)
               R = (T1 - entry) / risk / 2
           else:
               exit at the stop, R = -1.0
    2. Else if high >= T2:
           exit fully.
           R = ((T1 - entry)/risk + (T2 - entry)/risk) / 2
    3. Else if high >= T1 and half not yet booked:
           book HALF, move the stop UP to the ENTRY price, keep running.

    where risk = entry - original stop (the right shoulder low)

CRITICAL ORDERING RULE
    Within a single daily bar, the ADVERSE side is assumed to happen first.
    A bar whose low touches the stop AND whose high touches T2 is recorded as
    a STOP, R = -1.0.

    Daily bars do not tell you whether the high or the low came first.
    Assuming the favourable one would flatter every result. Do not change this.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE ARITHMETIC
    On the STLTECH reference, entry 700.00 against a 601.00 stop is a 14.1%
    stop. The R multiple is small relative to the capital at risk, and one
    loser costs what several partial winners make. Question Q9 is about this.

PART 5  ·  THE TWELVE OPEN QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each of these is currently a GUESS in the code. Something had to be chosen, so
something was. These are what we want your evidence on.

Q1.  WHICH SHOULDER FEEDS TARGET 1?
     The instruction said "first target is the length of the shoulder", but there are two
     shoulders of different lengths.
     NOW: the RIGHT shoulder (the one the stop sits under).
     On STLTECH: right gives T1 766.00, left would give 813.75.
     TEST: right vs left vs shallower vs average. Which maximises expectancy,
     and which maximises hit rate? They may not be the same answer.

Q2.  MUST THE NECKLINE BE HORIZONTAL?
     NOW: yes, always, drawn at the HIGHER of the two peaks.
     TEST: does allowing a sloping neckline (fitted through both peaks) find
     more real patterns, or just more noise? If sloping is allowed, what
     defines the break - a close above the projected line?

Q3.  HOW UNEQUAL MAY THE SHOULDERS BE?
     NOW: their DEPTHS below the neckline may differ by up to
     SHOULDER_TOL = 0.6 (60%).
     On STLTECH the left is 130.25 deep, the right 82.50 - a 37% difference.
     TEST: sweep this from 0.2 to 0.9. Does tightening it improve results, or
     just cut the sample? Is depth the right measure, or price level, or time?

Q4.  SYMMETRY IN TIME
     NOW: not checked at all. Only the total span is bounded
     (20-160 bars). A 10-bar left shoulder with a 90-bar
     right shoulder passes.
     TEST: add a time-symmetry constraint and see whether it helps.

Q5.  MUST THE HEAD BE THE LOWEST LOW IN THE WINDOW?
     NOW: no. The head only has to be lower than both shoulders, so a pattern
     can form partway through a longer decline with lower lows behind it.
     TEST: require the head to be the lowest low in the last N bars. Also:
     does the pattern need a prior DOWNTREND to be meaningful? Classical
     theory says it is a reversal pattern, which implies something to reverse.

Q6.  HOW FRESH MUST THE BREAKOUT BE?
     NOW: within FRESH_BARS = 3 daily bars.
     TEST: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10. How fast does the edge decay after the break?

Q7.  WHAT INVALIDATES A FORMING PATTERN?
     NOW: nothing. A shape sits in the "forming" list until it breaks out or
     falls out of the 200-bar window.
     TEST: does a close below the head, or below the right shoulder, kill the
     pattern? Should it expire after N bars without a break?

Q8.  RETEST OF THE NECKLINE
     NOW: ignored. Entry is the next open after the breakout close.
     TEST: compare (a) enter next open, vs (b) wait for a pullback to the
     neckline and enter there, vs (c) wait N bars for a retest then abandon.
     Report both expectancy AND how many signals each variant misses entirely,
     because a better entry that never fills is not better.

Q9.  THE STOP
     NOW: the right shoulder low. On STLTECH that is 14.1% below the entry.
     TEST: shoulder low vs just below the neckline vs a fixed percentage vs an
     ATR multiple. This is the single biggest lever on the results - report
     expectancy, win rate and max drawdown for each.

Q10. VOLUME - SHOULD IT COME BACK?
     NOW: measured, never enforced. Removed on instruction, but the
     original description said "good volume" and the reference chart broke out
     on only 1.09x.
     TEST: gate at 1.0x, 1.2x, 1.5x, 2.0x of the 20-day average. Report the
     trade count at each - a filter that improves win rate while removing 80%
     of signals may not be worth having.

Q11. THE PIVOT SIZE
     NOW: PIVOT_K = 5. A swing low is the lowest low within
     5 bars either side. This single number decides what counts as a
     shoulder at all.
     TEST: 3, 5, 7, 10. Expect this to change the sample size enormously.

Q12. ANYTHING WE ARE NOT LOOKING AT
     NOW: only price and volume from daily candles.
     TEST/SUGGEST: index trend at the time of the break, sector behaviour,
     position relative to a long moving average, the stock's own trend before
     the pattern, seasonality. If a condition you would always check by eye is
     missing, name it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 6  ·  TRAPS THIS PROJECT HAS ALREADY FALLEN INTO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Please read these. Each is easy to fall into and each will change your
conclusions rather than merely add noise.

6.1  UNDERPOWERED CONCLUSIONS
     The most common way a backtest of this kind goes wrong. A difference in
     average R between two variants will look real long before it is, and a
     bucket of 20 or 30 trades moves 0.3R when one trade changes.
     RULE: refuse to conclude on any group with n < 30. State the sample size
     beside every claim, and give a confidence interval rather than a point
     estimate. If the interval straddles zero, say so plainly.

6.2  THE COMPARISON THAT IS SECRETLY A DIFFERENT VARIABLE
     If you split trades into groups and the groups also differ in some other
     way - sector, price level, year, market regime, which sub-shape of the
     pattern they are - then you are not measuring what you think.
     RULE: before comparing two groups, list what else differs between them.

6.3  LOOK-AHEAD THROUGH A MUTATED FIELD
     These rules MOVE the stop to breakeven after T1. If your backtest stores
     one "stop" field and compares later prices against it, then for every
     trade that reached T1 you are comparing against the wrong level, and the
     error is invisible.
     RULE: keep the ORIGINAL stop separately. risk = entry - original stop,
     always. Reconstruct levels from the entry state, never from the final
     record.

6.4  BUILD IN A CROSS-CHECK BEFORE YOU TRUST THE SWEEP
     Reproduce the STLTECH trade in PART 1 and PART 3 by hand first: entry
     700.00, stop 601.00, T1 766.00, T2 874.75. If your replay does not
     produce those exact levels from the pattern, stop and find out why before
     running the whole universe. A single number that disagrees is worth more
     than a thousand rows that agree with each other.

6.5  SURVIVORSHIP AND DELISTING
     A universe built from TODAY's listed stocks and tested over past years
     silently excludes everything that delisted, merged or was suspended. On
     Indian smallcaps this is a large effect and it always flatters results.
     RULE: say plainly whether your universe is survivorship-biased. If it is,
     treat the result as an upper bound rather than an estimate.

6.6  CORPORATE ACTIONS
     Splits and bonuses on NSE smallcaps are frequent. Unadjusted data will
     manufacture enormous fake patterns - a 1:10 split looks like a 90% crash
     and will generate a textbook head.
     RULE: confirm your data is adjusted. Then eyeball the largest patterns
     your scan finds: if the head is a 90% drop, it is a split, not a pattern.

6.7  THE PRICE YOU COULD NOT HAVE HAD
     Entry must be the next day's OPEN. Using the breakout close books a price
     that only existed after the decision point.
     RULE: every price your backtest uses must have been observable at the
     moment the decision was made. Check each one against that test.

         the question

         your confidence: is this real, or inside the noise?
     One variable per block. Combined changes are not interpretable.

  D. YOUR RECOMMENDATION
     Which changes should go into the live code, in priority order. For each,
     the specific value (e.g. "PIVOT_K = 7", "stop = neckline - 0.5 x ATR14").
     We will implement these literally, so be precise.

  E. WHAT YOU COULD NOT TEST
     Anything you wanted to check and could not - missing data, ambiguity in
     these rules, questions this file does not answer. This is as useful as
     the results.

  F. CHARTS
     If possible: five patterns the detector ACCEPTED that you would have
     REJECTED by eye, and five it MISSED that you would have TAKEN. These are
     worth more than any statistic, because they show where the rules and an
     analyst's judgement diverge.
