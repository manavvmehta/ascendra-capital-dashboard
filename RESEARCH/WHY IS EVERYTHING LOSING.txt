================================================================================
   CHECK 1 — CAN MY TESTING FIND AN EDGE THAT IS KNOWN TO EXIST?
================================================================================

If my harness is broken it will fail to find something the published research
says IS there. So I ran MOMENTUM on the same data, same liquidity rule, same
costs: rank every liquid stock by its last twelve months' return skipping the
most recent month, buy the top tenth, hold a month, repeat. Nothing else.

   portfolio                        months   up    per month
   -------------------------------------------------------------
   TOP 10% - the momentum buy          24   54%       +Rs 417
   the whole liquid market             24   54%        +Rs 32
   BOTTOM 10% - the laggards           24   38%       -Rs 917

Winners beat the market, the market beats the laggards, and the gap is
+Rs 1,334 a month between the two ends. That is the textbook momentum result
and it came out of the same code that says the chart patterns lose.

So the machinery is not broken. It finds an edge when there is one.

(Honest caveat: only 24 rebalances, and the first half is -220 while the
second is +990. It is the right SHAPE, not yet a proven number.)


================================================================================
   CHECK 2 — ARE MY COSTS TOO HARSH?
================================================================================

Checked against Zerodha's actual published charges on a Rs 1,00,000 trade.

   INTRADAY   brokerage Rs 20 x 2, STT 0.025% on the sell, exchange
              0.00322% x 2, stamp 0.003%, SEBI, GST 18%
              = about Rs 83  ->  0.083%.   I used 0.082%.  Correct.

   DELIVERY   no brokerage, STT 0.1% on BOTH legs, exchange, stamp 0.015%,
              DP charge about Rs 15.93 on the sell, GST
              = about Rs 235  ->  0.235%.   I used 0.222%.

So the intraday figure is right, and the delivery figure I used is slightly
TOO KIND, not too harsh. Costs are not the reason the results are negative.


================================================================================
   CHECK 3 — ARE PEOPLE ACTUALLY MAKING MONEY DOING THIS?
================================================================================

This is the part worth sitting with, because it is not opinion. SEBI does not
survey volunteers - it counts every account.

   INTRADAY EQUITY, FY23 (SEBI, July 2024)
     71% of individual intraday traders LOST money
     80% lost among those trading more than 500 times a year
     76% lost among traders under 30
     loss-makers spent a further 57% of their losses on transaction costs
     profit-makers spent 19% of their profits on costs

   EQUITY F&O, FY22 to FY24 (SEBI, September 2024)
     93% of individual traders lost money
     aggregate losses over Rs 1.8 lakh crore in three years

Read the first line again. Seven in ten lose, and it gets worse the more you
trade. So the premise - that these strategies are widely making people money -
is not what the data shows. About three in ten intraday traders make money,
and only a fraction of those beat what they would have made just holding.


================================================================================
   SO WHY DOES IT LOOK LIKE EVERYONE IS WINNING?
================================================================================

Because you are only shown the winners, and that is not a figure of speech -
it is the difference between the two kinds of evidence above.

   SEBI counts everybody, including the ones who quit.
   Twitter and YouTube count volunteers, and nobody volunteers a bad year.

Out of a thousand people flipping coins, thirty will get ten heads in a row.
Those thirty will have a method, a course and a screenshot folder. The other
nine hundred and seventy stop posting. Both groups are real; only one is
visible.

This is also why hand-drawn backtests look so much better than mechanical
ones. Published mechanical tests of SMC produce win rates around 41%. The
screenshot backtests that circulate claim 70% and more. The difference is not
the method - it is knowing how the chart ended before choosing where the
pattern was.


================================================================================
   WHAT HAS ACTUALLY SURVIVED HONEST TESTING
================================================================================

Not nothing. But notice what the survivors have in common.

   MOMENTUM / TREND      The most replicated finding in finance, confirmed on
                         Indian equities in several published studies, and it
                         showed up in your own data above.

   WHAT IT IS NOT:       no chart pattern. No entry level. No stop. No
                         timeframe combination. It is a RANKING, held for a
                         month, rebalanced. You never look at a chart.

   CHART TRADING RULES   When researchers account for the fact that thousands
                         of rules get tried before one looks good, the
                         apparent profits mostly vanish - and the rules that
                         did work on pre-1990 data stopped working after
                         they were published.

That is the shape of the whole answer. The things that survive work on a slow
clock and need no chart reading. The things we have been testing work on a
fast clock and live or die inside a cost of 0.08% a trade while trying to
capture moves of 0.1% to 0.2%.


================================================================================
   WHERE I THINK WE ARE ACTUALLY GOING WRONG
================================================================================

Not the backtesting. The clock.

Every single thing tested in this project - the four setups, BREAKOUT-20D,
SMC, Fibonacci, the chart patterns, the candlesticks - asks the same question:
where does price go in the next few hours or days? At that horizon the move
you are trying to catch is only a few times bigger than what it costs to catch
it, so the method has to be right very often to survive. Nothing tested is.

Momentum asks a different question: which stocks have been strong for a year?
The answer moves slowly, the trade is held for a month, and the cost is a
rounding error against the move. That is why it survives testing and the chart
rules do not.

   HEIKEN ASHI, and the indicator question: Heiken Ashi is not new
   information. It is the same open, high, low and close, averaged. Averaging
   removes noise and adds LAG - it makes a trend easier to SEE after it has
   started. That can help a person stay in a trade; it cannot tell you
   anything the raw candles did not already contain. Twenty indicators were
   tested on your data on 10/09, leading and lagging, and every one subtracted
   from the base. I can test Heiken Ashi specifically if you want it, but I
   would not expect it to change the answer, and I would rather say that now
   than after spending a day on it.
