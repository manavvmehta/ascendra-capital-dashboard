THE RULE, identical for all six so the results mean the same thing

   the SIGNAL and the TARGET come from the strategy itself, unchanged
   the ENTRY and the STOP move to the small chart:
       wait for a pullback toward the level the signal is built on
       enter on the first small bar that closes beyond the previous one
       stop at the low of that pullback
       no entry within three big bars = MISSED, and counted as a miss

================================================================================
   NOT ONE OF THEM MAKES MONEY
================================================================================

Seventeen combinations, six strategies, and the best number in the whole study
is SMC on a 75-minute chart with 15-minute entries at -Rs 54 a trade. Nothing
crosses zero.


================================================================================
   THE USEFUL FINDING — WHERE THE IDEA HELPS AND WHERE IT HURTS
================================================================================

The combination is a STOP-WIDTH fix. That is all it is, and it explains every
row above.

   WHERE THE ONE-CHART STOP WAS WIDE, it helps enormously:

      chart patterns   stop 6.86% -> 1.12%    -Rs 976 -> -Rs 93
      Fibonacci        stop 7.59% -> 1.09%    -Rs 293 -> -Rs 195
      SMC              stop 2.33% -> 0.79%    -Rs 142 -> -Rs 62

   WHERE THE STOP WAS ALREADY TIGHT, it makes things WORSE:

      the four setups  stop 1.57% -> 0.88%     -Rs 43 -> -Rs 92
      BREAKOUT-20D     stop 2.91% -> 1.56%    -Rs 386 -> -Rs 473

Your four intraday setups already place a sensible stop. Tightening it again
on the 15-minute chart does not save money, it just gets you stopped out more
often - the win rate barely moves while the number of small losses climbs.

So the rule to keep is: use the small chart to fix a stop that is too wide.
Do not use it on a setup whose stop is already where it should be.

That is worth knowing and it is worth what it cost to find out.


================================================================================
   WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
================================================================================

It does not create an edge. It takes whatever direction the signal gives you
and lets you bet on it more cheaply. If the direction is worth nothing, a
cheaper bet on nothing is still worth nothing - it just loses more slowly,
which is exactly what every row above shows.

The measurement from this morning is the one that matters: with no stop and no
target at all, just holding, the signals still lose to a random entry in the
same stock. The problem is upstream of the stop.

ONE ROW WORTH A SECOND LOOK, honestly flagged rather than sold: SMC at
-Rs 54 and -Rs 62 is the closest anything has come. But 93% of its signals
never fill - 170 trades out of about 2,800 signals - so the sample is thin,
and the two halves of the year disagree (-8 then -116 on one pair, -87 then
-21 on the other). It is not evidence yet. It is a reason to keep recording.
