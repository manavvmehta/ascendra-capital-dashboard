================================================================================
   CHART PATTERNS ON NSE  —  DO THEY WORK?
   Tested 11/09/2026 for ASCENDRA CAPITAL
================================================================================

WHAT WAS TESTED

  16 classical chart patterns, detected mechanically:

    reversal       head and shoulders, inverted head and shoulders,
                   double top, double bottom, triple top, triple bottom,
                   cup with handle
    continuation   ascending / descending / symmetrical triangles,
                   bull flag, bear flag, pennant,
                   rising wedge, falling wedge, rectangle

  Daily      3 years, 2,330 stocks, 11,133 trades
  15-minute  1 year, 398 stocks, 58,112 trades


WHERE THE RULES CAME FROM

  head and shoulders      Bulkowski, thepatternsite.com
  everything else         StockCharts ChartSchool

  Numbers were used as published - double top peaks "within 3%", the trough
  "at least 10%" deep, the handle retracing "up to 1/3 of the cup", the
  symmetrical triangle breaking between half and three quarters of the way to
  the apex. Nothing was tuned afterwards to make a result look better.


HOW THE TRADES WERE TAKEN

  ENTRY    the NEXT bar's open after a bar CLOSED beyond the trigger level.
           No intrabar fills.
  STOP     where the pattern puts it - right shoulder, second peak, far side
           of the triangle, the handle's low.
  TARGET   the textbook measured move.
  INSIDE   a bar that touches both the stop and the target is scored as the
           STOP, always.
  COSTS    daily 0.222% (equity delivery, STT on both legs); shorts held
           overnight only in the 216 stocks that have futures, at 0.07%.
           15-minute 0.082%, squared off at 15:10.
  LIQUID   median turnover of the previous 20 sessions at least Rs 5 crore.

  THE CONTROL - and this is what decides everything. Every pattern trade is
  matched against a coin toss: the same side, held the same length of time,
  in the same stock on a random day AND in a different stock on the same day.
  A pattern has to beat BOTH. If it does not, the shape on the chart added
  nothing and you were simply long a rising market.

  LOOK-AHEAD AUDIT: 1,100 patterns were re-detected with the chart cut off at
  the bar that completed them. All 1,100 were still found with no future bars
  visible. Nothing in this test used hindsight.


================================================================================
   THE ANSWER
================================================================================

  Not one of the sixteen patterns makes money on NSE, on either timeframe,
  after costs, against its own control.

  On daily bars every single pattern loses. The whole book is -Rs 440 a trade
  while a random entry in the same stocks made +Rs 201. Buying on a coin toss
  beat every pattern except the short ones, and the short ones lost too.

  On 15-minute bars everything sits on zero: -Rs 41 a trade against -Rs 49 for
  the coin toss. The two that look positive (double bottom +Rs 123, triple
  bottom +Rs 62) do not survive the significance bar, which with 32 tests is
  p < 0.0016, and both were flat in the first half of the year.

  It is NOT the stop being too tight. Widening the stop to 3x helps some
  patterns - bull flag goes from -Rs 261 to +Rs 1,107 - but the coin toss over
  the same period made +Rs 3,691. Wider stops close the gap to random; they
  never cross it.

  And it is NOT the exit rules either. With NO stop and NO target at all, just
  holding for 40 days, the pattern entries STILL lose to a random entry in the
  same stock in 13 of 16 cases. The entry itself carries no information.

================================================================================
   WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
================================================================================

  It does not say the patterns are imaginary. They are there on the chart and
  the detector finds thousands of them. It says that once you pay Indian
  costs, and once you compare against simply buying something on the same
  morning, the shape adds nothing.

  Bulkowski's own numbers look far better than this - 19% failure on a head
  and shoulders top, 16% average decline. Three differences explain the gap,
  and they are worth knowing:

    1. His patterns are hand-picked from US charts. A person choosing which
       ones "look right" is doing something the detector cannot copy - and
       that judgement may be where the money actually is.
    2. He measures to the ultimate high or low, not to a stop and a target.
       A trade with no stop and infinite patience is not a trade.
    3. He does not charge costs. At 0.222% a round trip on delivery, an edge
       of a few tenths of a percent is gone before it starts.

  The honest reading: if someone makes money with chart patterns, it is
  probably not the pattern doing it. It is which ones they choose to take,
  what they refuse, how big they go, and when they sit out.


   WHAT I WOULD NOT DO NEXT

  Keep cutting the data. Sixteen patterns x two timeframes is already 32
  tests; slice each one by time of day, stop width and volume and something
  will come out positive by luck alone. That is how the last four backtests
  in this project nearly fooled us.
